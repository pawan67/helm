The bar node is a breadboard build around an ESP32. Only five things are truly
required; the IR blaster is a bonus you can add later.

<Callout type="warn">
Always unplug the ESP32 from USB while you wire. Plug power in only after you've
double-checked a connection — a swapped `+`/`GND` can kill a part.
</Callout>

## Bill of materials [#parts]

| # | Part | What it does | Needed? |
|---|------|--------------|---------|
| 1 | ESP32 dev board (DevKitC / WROOM) | The brain | ✅ required |
| 2 | VL53L0X ToF distance sensor | Detects reps / hang | ✅ required |
| 3 | DHT11 temp/humidity sensor | Room climate | ✅ required |
| 4 | **Passive** piezo buzzer | Chirps on reps | ✅ required |
| 5 | IR LED + 2N2222 transistor + 470 µF cap | Controls AC / fan / TV | ⭕ optional |
| — | Resistors: **10 kΩ**, **220 Ω**, **~47 Ω** | see [Resistors](/docs/hardware/resistors) | ✅ / ⭕ |
| — | Breadboard + jumper wires | Connections | ✅ |

<Callout type="info" title="Passive, not active buzzer">
The firmware plays tones with `tone()`. A **passive** piezo has no sticker over
the hole and clicks (not beeps) if you touch its pins to 3V3. An active buzzer
just buzzes one note — the wrong part here.
</Callout>

## What each part is for

- **ESP32** — runs the detection state machine, reads the sensors, drives the
  buzzer and IR, and talks to the server over WiFi + MQTT.
- **VL53L0X** — a time-of-flight laser distance sensor on I²C. Mounted above the
  bar pointing at your head, it turns "how close is your head" into reps and
  hang time.
- **DHT11** — a cheap temperature + humidity sensor for the room climate charts.
- **Passive buzzer** — chirps on each counted rep and plays a jingle on session
  end (both toggleable in Settings).
- **IR blaster** (optional) — an IR LED driven by a transistor so it's bright
  enough to reach your AC / fan / TV across the room.

## Where the sensor goes

Mount the VL53L0X on the wall **10 cm above the bar**, centered, lens pointing
**outward toward your body** — so your head enters the beam as you pull up. When
you hang, your head reads far/low in the beam; at the top of a pull-up your head
rises close to the sensor.

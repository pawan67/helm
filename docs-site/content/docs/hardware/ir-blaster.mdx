The IR blaster lets HELM control an AC, fan, or TV over infrared. It's entirely
optional — the tracker works fine without it. Skip this whole page if you don't
want IR control.

<Callout type="warn">
This part has **real polarity** on three components (IR LED, transistor, and the
electrolytic capacitor). An electrolytic cap can pop if reversed — double-check
before powering on.
</Callout>

## Why a transistor?

The ESP32 can't drive a bright IR LED string directly, so a **2N2222 transistor**
switches the LED from the 5 V rail while the ESP32 just toggles the base.

```text
  ESP32 GPIO27 ──[220Ω]──► B (base)
                            │
             5V ──► IR LED ──[47Ω]──► C (collector)
                  (anode +) (cathode −)      │
                                    E (emitter) ──► GND

  470µF capacitor across the 5V supply:  +leg → 5V,  −leg(stripe) → GND
```

## Polarity, part by part

- **IR LED** — **long leg = anode (+)** → toward `5V`. Short leg / **flat side of
  the rim = cathode (−)** → toward the 47 Ω resistor and the collector.
- **2N2222 transistor** (TO-92, flat face toward you, legs down) — the most common
  order is **E – B – C** (left → right): left leg = Emitter → `GND`, middle = Base
  → 220 Ω → `GPIO27`, right = Collector → the IR LED side.

  <Callout type="warn">
  Pinouts vary by brand. Confirm E–B–C against your transistor's datasheet or
  marking before wiring.
  </Callout>

- **470 µF capacitor** — **longer leg = `+`** → `5V`. The side with the **stripe /
  minus marks = `−`** → `GND`. It buffers the burst current when the LED pulses.

## Sizing the LED resistor

Put the IR LEDs on the **`5V`** rail (not `3V3`) for range. Size the current-limit
resistor for your LED count — roughly **22–47 Ω** for one or two LEDs at 5 V. A
single 470 µF cap across the supply smooths the burst draw.

Point the IR LEDs at the AC / fan / TV you want to control — it needs
line-of-sight.

## IR receiver — optional (for "learn" mode) [#receiver]

Rather than hunting for hex codes, you can have HELM **learn** a code straight off
your existing remote. Add a **38 kHz IR receiver** — a 3-pin part (VS1838B, or a
TSOP38238 module) that looks like a little black LED. It wires to **`GPIO14`** and
needs **no resistor or transistor** — it's a powered module with a logic output.

<Callout type="warn">
A bare receiver has **no printed labels** — its pins go **by position**, and
swapping `VCC`/`GND` can **destroy** the part. Confirm the order against your
exact part's datasheet before powering on.
</Callout>

**VS1838B pinout** — hold it with the **rounded (dome / lens) side facing you** and
the **legs pointing down**, then read left → right:

```text
   VS1838B — dome toward you, legs down:

     OUT       GND       VCC
      │         │         │
   GPIO14      GND       3V3   (3.3 V — NOT 5 V)
```

- **Left leg = `OUT`** → `GPIO14`
- **Middle leg = `GND` (−)** → `GND`
- **Right leg = `VCC` (+)** → `3V3`

If it's wired right, `OUT` idles near 3.3 V and dips low when it sees IR. Point
the dome at whatever remote you'll press.

### Using learn mode

<Steps>

<Step>
Flash firmware **v1.7.0+** (adds receiver support) — over USB or [OTA](/docs/firmware/ota).
</Step>

<Step>
On the console's **Remote** page, hit **Edit**, then **Add** a button on any
device — including the **AC** card.
</Step>

<Step>
Click **Learn from remote**, point your remote at the bar node, and press the
button you want (e.g. the AC's **Light**). Protocol, code and bits fill in
automatically. Name it and **Save**.
</Step>

</Steps>

The receiver's interrupt is enabled **only during that short learn window** (it
auto-stops after ~20 s), so it never disturbs rep detection the rest of the time.
Buttons you add to the **AC** card are also published to Home Assistant, so a
learned "lights off" works by voice too.

## What the firmware does with it

Once wired and configured, the firmware subscribes to an IR command topic and
transmits either a Panasonic AC state frame or a generic protocol code (NEC, etc.).
